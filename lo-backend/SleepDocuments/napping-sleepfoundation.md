# Does Napping Impact Sleep at Night?

Source: https://www.sleepfoundation.org/how-sleep-works/does-napping-impact-sleep-at-night

Naps don't always hurt night-time sleep. It mostly comes down to when you nap and for how long. Short naps earlier in the day rarely cause problems, but long naps or naps late in the afternoon can make it harder to fall asleep and stay asleep at night. People who already struggle with insomnia are more sensitive to this.

Well-timed afternoon naps don't seem to harm night sleep. In several studies, people who napped in the afternoon got the same or more total sleep across 24 hours as people who didn't. The catch is that napping lowers your sleep drive, the body's built-up need for sleep. A nap that's too late or too long leaves less sleep drive for bedtime. That's why experts usually say to avoid napping after 3pm, or at least 8 hours before bed.

The best time for most people with a normal schedule is early afternoon, roughly 1pm to 3pm, during the natural "post-lunch dip" in energy. A nap then can boost energy, alertness, mood, memory, thinking and physical performance.

For most people, 30 minutes or less is ideal. Short naps keep you in light sleep. Waking from deep sleep can leave you groggier than before the nap. A 90-minute nap can also work, because it lets you go through a full sleep cycle without being woken mid-deep sleep.

Tips for a good nap:
- Set an alarm so a short nap doesn't turn into a long one.
- Nap in the early afternoon rather than late in the day.
- Block out light and noise with blackout curtains, an eye mask, earplugs or white noise.
- Try a "coffee nap": drinking coffee just before a short nap helps you feel more alert afterwards than a nap alone.

If you find yourself napping a lot, longer and more often, it may mean you aren't getting enough or good-enough sleep at night. If you still need naps even when you sleep enough, see a doctor to check for a sleep disorder.

Naps do count towards your total sleep, and they can help you feel better after a bad night. But they don't replace proper sleep. A nap is usually too short to go through all the sleep stages, including REM sleep, so four two-hour naps aren't as good as eight hours in one go. A quick nap of around 20 minutes is still a useful recharge when you feel groggy.
