# Irregular Sleep-Wake Rhythm Disorder

Source: https://www.sleepfoundation.org/circadian-rhythm-sleep-disorders/irregular-sleep-wake-rhythm-disorder

Irregular sleep-wake rhythm disorder is a rare circadian rhythm disorder where a person has no clear sleep pattern. Instead of one main sleep at night, they sleep and wake at random times across the 24 hours, and the pattern changes from day to day. Typical signs are trouble sleeping at night, waking often, sleep that doesn't feel refreshing, heavy daytime sleepiness and several naps of two to four hours spread across the day.

The disorder is very uncommon. It mostly shows up in people with conditions that affect the brain, such as dementia, Parkinson's or Huntington's disease, autism, childhood developmental disorders, traumatic brain injury, brain tumours or chronic fatigue syndrome. Too little daylight, exercise and social contact during the day, or too much light at night, can also make it more likely. A doctor quoted in the article notes that for most people with poor sleep or daytime sleepiness, a much more common cause is to blame, so the first step is to talk to a doctor rather than assume this disorder.

Doctors diagnose it with one to two weeks of sleep diaries or a wrist-worn movement tracker. Treatment aims to line sleep back up with day and night, using morning bright light therapy, better sleep habits and sometimes medicine or melatonin under a doctor's guidance.

The habits recommended for this disorder are useful for anyone whose sleep times jump around:
- Go to bed and get up at the same times every day, including weekends, and build a relaxing bedtime routine.
- Don't lie in bed struggling to sleep. If you're still awake after about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm and try again later.
- Get plenty of daylight during the day, and cut down on artificial light and screens at night.
- Limit alcohol, which causes more waking in the night and more sleepiness the next day.
- Avoid lots of naps, especially in the late afternoon, because they break up night-time sleep.
- Caffeine can help with daytime sleepiness, but too much, or any in the late afternoon or within about 8 hours of bed, can cause trouble sleeping.
- Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and cool.

Not getting enough sleep over a long time is linked to accidents, heart disease, high blood pressure, depression, obesity and diabetes, so see a doctor if you're regularly short on sleep or struggling to function during the day.
