# Tips for Shift Workers
Source: https://www.sleepfoundation.org/shift-work-disorder/tips

(Summary written in our own words, not the original article text.)

Any job with hours outside roughly 7am to 6pm counts as shift work. This includes fixed night hours, rotating shifts, split shifts and irregular rosters. Night, early morning and rotating shifts make good sleep hard, and some workers end up with shift work disorder: trouble sleeping when they try to rest and heavy tiredness on the job. This can hurt thinking, health and work performance, and makes mistakes and accidents more likely.

The most important habit for night workers is keeping the same sleep and wake times, including on days off. If you normally sleep from 8am after a shift and get up at 5pm, try to keep that pattern on your days off too, even though it's hard. Let the people you live with know when your sleep time is so they don't disturb you.

Daytime sleep is easily broken by light and noise. Use blackout blinds or an eye mask, earplugs or a white noise machine, and switch your phone off if you aren't on call.

Some shift workers stay up for a few hours after getting home so they wake closer to their next shift. Others split their sleep, with a nap after getting home and a longer sleep before the next shift. Try different setups and see what works for you.

Wind down before bed with a warm shower, meditation or something relaxing. Alcohol might make you fall asleep faster, but it breaks up your sleep later as your body processes it. Talk to a doctor before trying melatonin, because it can shift your body clock.

Staying alert at work:

- Have caffeine early in the shift, in small amounts spread out, rather than a lot at once. It kicks in after about 15 to 20 minutes. Stop caffeine 3 to 4 hours before you plan to sleep.
- A short burst of exercise on a break, like a quick walk, can give you an energy boost.
- A nap of 10 to 20 minutes on a break helps without leaving you groggy. A "coffee nap" means drinking coffee right before a 15 to 20 minute nap, so the caffeine kicks in as you wake up.
- Tired shift workers, new shift workers and people on longer shifts than usual are more likely to make mistakes, so take extra care.
- Drowsy driving is a big risk, especially between midnight and 6am. Nap briefly before driving home, and pull over somewhere safe to nap if you feel sleepy on the road.

Most adults need at least 7 hours of sleep a day, so finding a routine that gets you there may take some trial and error.

Rotating shifts are harder on sleep than fixed shifts. Before switching from days to nights, push your bedtime back by an hour or two each night for a few days. Rotating forward (day, then afternoon, then night) is easier on the body than going backwards or rotating randomly. Changing shifts every 2 to 3 days can be easier than every 5 to 7 days, and long runs of night shifts are tough. If your roster is wearing you down, ask your supervisor whether it can be changed.
